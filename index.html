Essentials

Currency

Polish złoty (PLN). You can pay by card nearly everywhere – just note that some places don't accept American Express, so bring a Visa or Mastercard too.

Language

Polish. You will have no problem communicating in English in most places.

Power

Type C/E plugs, 230 V. Depending on where you're travelling from, you might need an adapter.

Tipping

A tip of around 10% is customary in restaurants, though not obligatory. For groups of more than 6, many restaurants add a service charge to the bill automatically, so check before tipping.

Time zone

Central European Time (CET/CEST).

Weather & what to wear

October in Kraków is usually quite sunny during the day, with afternoons reaching around 21 °C. The difference between day and night is big, though: mornings and evenings can be 10–15 °C colder than midday.

Loading live forecast…

Suggested outfit

  • Layers: a T-shirt or shirt with a sweater or fleece you can take off at midday.
  • A light to medium jacket for chilly mornings and evenings out.
  • Comfortable walking shoes. The Old Town has cobblestones, and the Salt Mine route is about 3.5 km.
  • A warm layer for the Salt Mine, which stays at 17–18 °C underground.
  • A small umbrella, just in case.

Avoid unmarked taxis waiting at the airport or the Main Square. Use an app or the official taxi rank.

Airport (KRK, Balice)

  • Train: direct train from the airport to Kraków Główny (main station), about 20 minutes.
  • Taxi / app: about 25–40 minutes to the centre, depending on traffic.

Public transport

  • Trams and buses cover the whole city; tickets are sold in machines at stops, on board (card accepted) and via apps.
  • Use jakdojade.pl (website or app) to find routes on all public transport, get step-by-step directions and buy tickets.
  • The Old Town, Wawel and Kazimierz are all within comfortable walking distance of the hotel.

Places to see

Main Market Square

The heart of the Old Town, with the Cloth Hall and St. Mary's Basilica.

Sukiennice (Cloth Hall)

The Renaissance trading hall in the middle of the Main Square. Browse amber, crafts and souvenirs on the ground floor, see 19th-century Polish art upstairs, or explore the Rynek Underground museum below.

Wawel Royal Castle

Castle and cathedral on the hill by the Vistula. A short walk from the hotel.

Kazimierz

Historic Jewish quarter with cafés, galleries and lively evening spots.

Schindler's Factory

Museum on wartime Kraków in Zabłocie. Book tickets in advance.

Vistula Boulevards

Riverside walk below Wawel, nice at sunset.

Unique Kraków experiences

Kiełbaski z Niebieskiej Nyski

A Kraków cult classic: grilled sausages served with bread and mustard straight from an old blue Nysa van (a communist-era delivery van). It parks by Hala Targowa in the evenings and serves until late at night, a favourite last stop after a night out.

Show on map

Zapiekanki at Okrąglak

A zapiekanka is a half baguette topped with mushrooms and melted cheese, finished with ketchup and many other toppings. The best-known ones come from the windows of Okrąglak, the round market hall in the middle of Plac Nowy in Kazimierz. They are open until late.

Show on map

Horse carriage or melex ride

Two classic ways to see the city without walking:

Horse-drawn carriage: a romantic ride around the Old Town and down to Wawel. Carriages wait on the Main Square.

Melex: a small electric cart tour of the Old Town, Kazimierz and the former Jewish ghetto, with an audio guide in many languages.

Both start around the Main Square. Show on map

Kraków Balloon

A tethered sightseeing balloon that rises to about 150 m, with sweeping views over Wawel, the Old Town and Kazimierz. The gondola fits around 30 people. Flights depend on the weather.

Bulwar Wołyński, by the Vistula, just across the river from your hotel.
Show on map

Cracow Eye

A 50 m observation wheel on the riverbank, with a great panorama of Wawel and the city. It is especially nice at sunset.

Bulwar Wołyński, next to the balloon. Show on map

The fire-breathing Wawel Dragon

According to legend, a dragon once lived in a cave under Wawel Hill. Today a bronze statue of the dragon stands at the entrance to its den by the river and breathes real fire every few minutes. It is a fun, free stop on a riverside walk.

Foot of Wawel Hill, a short walk from your hotel. Show on map
